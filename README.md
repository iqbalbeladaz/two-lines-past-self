## The entry

`main.py` writes exactly two lines to `/output/entry.txt` — line one warns the
writer's past self about one specific moment in a single sentence, line two
says in one sentence why that moment mattered and what changed because of it:

> Don't throw away the letter from Diponegoro on 14 March 2019 — open it; it is an acceptance, not a rejection.
> It sat in the bin for a week, and by the time you fished it out, your place had gone to someone else.

212 characters, two lines, deterministic: the program writes the same bytes
on every run.

## Why this text

- **One concrete moment:** a dated decision — the letter from Diponegoro on
  14 March 2019, thrown away unopened.
- **A twist that sharpens it:** the letter was an acceptance, not a rejection.
- **Real cause and effect:** the letter sat in the bin for a week, and by the
  time it was fished out, the place had gone to someone else — the second line
  traces the consequence instead of restating the warning.
- **Plain, vivid voice:** written to one specific person, no wasted words.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes /output/entry.txt
```
